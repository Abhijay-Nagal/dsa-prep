Running it

From the project folder:

npm installOnce, after cloning.
npm run devDevelopment server on http://localhost:3000.
npm run buildProduction build. Prerenders every page.
npm startServes the production build. Offline mode works here.

Needs Node 20 or newer. There is no backend, no database and no environment variables to set.

Install it as an app. Open it in Chrome or Edge and use the install button in the address bar, or accept the in-app prompt. It then opens in its own window, works with no network, and picks up four shortcuts: today's plan, the review queue, visualisers and Blind 75.

First run

A five-step wizard appears the first time. It is worth two minutes, because the answers seed the learning engine.

  1. Your name — only used to greet you, and optional.
  2. Your level — sets your starting rating, so the first recommendations are not random.
  3. Target company — reweights every topic towards that company's interview style. Skippable.
  4. Runway — how long until your interview. This picks your starting phase.
  5. Daily minutes — the planner sizes each day to exactly this budget.

All five are editable later from Profile and from the dashboard.

The daily loop

Open the Dashboard and work top to bottom. It is designed so you never have to decide what to do next.

Daily challenge

One problem, date-seeded

The same for everyone, rotating by weekday: easy on Sunday, hard on Wednesday and Saturday, medium otherwise. Solve it, then claim the bonus XP.

Today's plan

Reviews first, then new work

Due reviews come first, the remaining minutes are filled with the highest-value problems that fit, and one deliberate stretch problem is added at the end.

Goal ring

Problems solved today

Counts towards your streak. Streaks carry two freezes, and each absorbs one missed day.

Recommended next

Scored, not shuffled

Each card says why it was picked: pattern mastery, your rating, interview frequency, your target company.

Company sheets

Pick a company from Companies and you get its real round structure, what its bar looks like, its difficulty mix, a reordered problem list, and a readiness score out of 100 that compares your mastery and rating against that company's tier. Service-company pages (TCS, Infosys, Wipro and similar) are tuned for online assessment rounds rather than full onsite loops.

Set one as your target and the whole app reorders around it: recommendations, the daily plan and the weakest-skills list all shift towards that company's focus topics.

Progress, XP and streaks

Solving awards XP with a visible breakdown: difficulty, a no-hint bonus, a first-try bonus, your streak multiplier, and a speed bonus for finishing inside the estimate. XP drives 24 levels, each with its own title, and there are 42 achievements across four tiers — three of them secret.

Behind all of it, four models run on your history: a spaced-repetition scheduler, Bayesian knowledge tracing per topic and per pattern, an Elo rating for you against every problem, and a planner that fills each day with a knapsack over your remaining minutes. None of them need a server.

Working a problem

Every problem page has the same five parts.

Interview mode A timed round scaled to the problem's target time, with phase prompts: clarify, plan out loud, code, verify, wrap up. Finish as solved or attempted and the time is recorded either way.
Hint ladder Hints reveal one at a time, from a nudge to the full approach. Using fewer hints earns more XP and counts as stronger evidence of mastery.
Approach The intended solution, its complexity, and a follow-up an interviewer would ask next.
Scratchpad A code editor with syntax highlighting. JavaScript actually runs, in a sandboxed worker with a timeout.
Notes Saved as you type. These collect in the Notebook and are the best revision material in the app.
Find the same problem elsewhere. Each page links out to LeetCode, GeeksforGeeks, Code360, InterviewBit, HackerRank, CodeChef and a video search. Links are marked direct when the exact problem is known and search otherwise, so you always know which is which.
Getting back. Every detail page has a Back button, and next to it a named chip linking straight to the list that sent you there. The breadcrumb beside them is a category path, so the middle crumb on a problem takes you to that topic in Learn, which is deliberate rather than a way back.

Spaced repetition

Solving a problem schedules it for review. The scheduler tracks a memory stability per problem and brings it back when your predicted recall falls to about 90 percent — the point where one look is enough and any later would mean relearning it from scratch.

Open Revise and grade each card honestly: Forgot, Hard, Good or Easy. Grading low is not a failure, it just pulls the card closer. Reviews count towards your streak.

Seeing the algorithms

Forty-six step-by-step animations, all with editable input.

Visualise

One algorithm, frame by frame

Play, pause, step, scrub and replay at seven speeds. Each frame narrates what just happened, highlights the pseudocode line being executed, and updates live counters. Type your own input and watch it run on that.

Algorithm race

Two algorithms, same input

Pick a matchup — bubble against quick, BFS against DFS — and step both in lockstep. The verdict says how many times fewer steps the winner took. Steps are animation frames, so they track comparisons and visits rather than nanoseconds.

The other tools

Roadmap The 24 topics as a prerequisite graph, seven layers deep. Nodes fill as your mastery grows, and a lock means a prerequisite is not ready. Hover any topic to trace what it needs and what it unlocks.
Complexity
explorer		 Drag n and watch which growth classes fall off the cliff. The constraint decoder turns n ≤ 10^5 into a shortlist of algorithms that will actually pass — the single most useful reflex in a live round.
Notebook Every note you have written, everything you starred, every problem you attempted but did not finish, and your recent history. Searchable, editable in place, and exportable as one Markdown file.
Arena Three games: a timed mock round drawn from a company's real difficulty mix, a pattern-recognition sprint, and a complexity reading drill.
Focus timer The pill in the bottom-left corner. Sessions of 25 or 50 minutes with breaks, and it keeps running while you browse. Completed sessions log study minutes towards your streak.
Learn Notes per topic and per pattern: the key ideas, the trigger phrases that give the pattern away, a code template and a generic hint ladder.
Analytics Mastery radar, rating history, an activity heatmap and difficulty breakdowns.

DSA Prep — user guideTools and shortcuts

Your data

Everything — progress, notes, code, settings, streak — lives in this browser's local storage under one key, dsa-prep-v1. Nothing is uploaded anywhere, and there is no account to create.

  • Moving devices: Profile → Export downloads a JSON file. Import it on the other device.
  • Same browser: your streak only counts if you use the same browser profile each day.
  • Starting over: Profile → Reset clears progress. It cannot be undone, so export first.
  • Private windows: storage is cleared when the window closes, so progress will not persist.

A suggested first week

  1. Finish the wizard. Pick the runway that is actually true, not the one you wish were true.
  2. Do the daily challenge, then whatever the plan lists. Do not skip ahead — the order is doing work.
  3. Write one line of notes on every problem, even the easy ones. Future you reads those, not the solution.
  4. When a problem confuses you, open its visualiser before you read the approach.
  5. Clear the review queue daily. It is short by design, and skipping it is what makes phase two feel hard.
  6. On day seven, open the Roadmap and Analytics. Both will show you what to fix without you having to guess.
One honest caveat. Problem metadata is curated, not scraped. Interview frequency is a judgement call on a five-point scale, company tags are reported tags where those are known, and round structures reflect publicly reported processes that drift over time. Treat the numbers as a good prior, not as ground truth.
